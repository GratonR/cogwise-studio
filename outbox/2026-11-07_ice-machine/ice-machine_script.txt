This machine makes ice by freezing water, then blasting it with heat.
The secret: it never fills a tray. It freezes a waterfall.
A pump lifts water and pours it over an ice-cold metal plate.
Inside the plate, refrigerant boils and pulls the heat out of the water.
Ice grows in each little cell, one thin layer at a time.
Air and minerals stay in the moving water and wash away.
That's why the cubes come out crystal clear.
When the ice is thick enough, a probe ends the freeze.
A valve sends hot refrigerant gas into the plate. The cubes slip off and drop.
Running water, a cold plate, then a blast of heat: clear ice.
