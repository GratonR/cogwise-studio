In 1755, a Scottish doctor made ice by making a liquid boil.
The secret: boiling doesn't need heat from a flame. It steals heat.
William Cullen put ether in a glass, then pumped the air out above it.
With less pressure pushing down, the ether boiled at room temperature.
To turn into vapour, every gram drags heat out of whatever it touches.
The glass got so cold that the water around it froze.
Same as sweat: one gram of water evaporating carries off over two thousand joules.
Problem: the vapour escaped into the room, and the ether was gone.
In 1834, Jacob Perkins closed the loop with a pump that squeezes the vapour.
Squeezed, it turns back into liquid and dumps the heat outside, behind your fridge.
Your fridge does this nonstop: its liquid boils at minus twelve Celsius, eleven Fahrenheit.
Recap: a fridge is a liquid boiling inside it, forever recycled.
