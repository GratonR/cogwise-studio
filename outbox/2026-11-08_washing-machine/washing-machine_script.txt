There are about twenty-five kilos of concrete inside your washing machine. Fifty-five pounds.
Under the lid: one block on the tub's top, another at its front.
Because the tub isn't bolted down. It hangs on springs and sits on shock absorbers.
At full spin, the drum turns about fourteen hundred times a minute.
But wet clothes never spread evenly. One side of the drum is always heavier.
That lump swings around and tugs the tub sideways, over twenty times a second.
Heavy things are hard to shake, so concrete turns a hard tug into a wobble.
The springs let the tub float, and the shock absorbers soak up what's left.
Without that weight, the whole machine would hop and walk across your floor.
Why concrete? It's cheap, dense, and does nothing but sit there being heavy.
Modern plastic tubs are lighter than old steel ones, so concrete adds the weight back.
So the calmest part of your washer is a block of concrete.
