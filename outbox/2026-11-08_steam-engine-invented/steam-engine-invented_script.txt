That pot lid that sticks when it cools? That's how the steam engine started.
Inside, the steam turns back into a few drops of water.
Steam takes up over a thousand times more room, so almost nothing is left.
Now the air outside presses the lid down. It's stuck.
Air presses about one kilo on every square centimetre: up to three hundred kilos on this lid. Seven hundred pounds.
Now swap the lid for a piston in a tall tube, roped to a bucket.
Boil a little water underneath. Steam lifts the piston, and a latch holds it.
Take away the fire. The steam turns back to water and leaves a vacuum.
Release the latch. The air slams the piston down, and the bucket shoots up.
No steam pushed that bucket. The weight of the air did.
Denis Papin built exactly this in 1690. Newcomen scaled it up to pump flooded mines.
Steam makes the vacuum. Air does the work. It all started with a lid.
