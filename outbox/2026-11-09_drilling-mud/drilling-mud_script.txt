Nobody can see three kilometers underground. So drillers read what the mud brings up.
The secret: this mud is a conveyor belt from the bottom of the hole.
Pumps push it down inside the drill pipe and out through the bit's jets.
Then it climbs back up around the pipe, carrying the chips the bit just cut.
From three kilometers, nearly two miles down, that trip up can take a whole hour.
At the top, a shaking screen catches the chips, and a geologist names the rock under a microscope.
Under ultraviolet light, rock that holds oil starts to glow.
Gas rides up too. A spinning beater in a gas trap shakes it loose.
A detector counts it, methane to pentane, every minute or two.
A sudden jump in gas, plus glowing chips? Drillers call that an oil show.
Mud goes down, rock and gas come up. The hole reports on itself, meter by meter.
