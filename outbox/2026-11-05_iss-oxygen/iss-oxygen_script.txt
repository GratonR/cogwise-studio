No windows to open, 400 kilometers up. So where does the air come from?
Here's the secret: the station makes its own oxygen, out of plain water.
The trick? Run electricity through water, and it splits into oxygen and hydrogen.
It starts with water, recycled from humidity, sweat, and even the crew's urine.
Solar panels power the electrolyzer. Oxygen bubbles out and flows into the cabin.
Every breath adds carbon dioxide. Zeolite beads trap it from the air.
Zeolite is a mineral crystal of silicon, aluminum and oxygen, full of molecule-sized tunnels.
Carbon dioxide sticks to the tunnel walls, while oxygen and nitrogen slip right through.
Heat the beads, and they let the carbon dioxide go again, ready to be reused.
In a hot reactor, that carbon dioxide meets the leftover hydrogen from the electrolyzer.
Out come water and methane. The water goes back to make more oxygen.
The methane is useless on board, so it's simply vented out into space.
But methane carries hydrogen away, so only about half that oxygen comes back.
That's why cargo ships still bring water and oxygen, with chemical oxygen candles as backup.
So the air on the station is mostly yesterday's water, split by sunlight.
Well done! Now you know where astronauts get every single breath. Pretty clever, right?
