This heat pump dryer dries your clothes… with a cold metal coil.
The trick: warm air can hold far more water than cold air.
Warm air from twenty to fifty Celsius, sixty-eight to one twenty-two Fahrenheit: it holds five times more water.
First, a hot coil warms the air, and a fan blows it through your clothes.
The warm air soaks up the water. Without heat, it would fill up fast and drying would stall.
Then that soggy air is pushed across a cold coil.
Cold air can't hold all that water, so it rains out as droplets, just like on a cold glass in summer.
The drops run into a tank or a drain hose. No air goes outside, so no hole in the wall.
Both coils form that heat pump, the same loop as your fridge.
Heat pulled out at the cold coil goes straight back into the hot one.
So your clothes stay cooler, and it uses much less electricity.
Heat makes the air thirsty. Cold wrings it out. Same air, round and round.
