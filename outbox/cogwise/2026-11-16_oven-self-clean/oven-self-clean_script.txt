Press one button and your oven locks itself shut, then turns grease into ash.
It's called pyrolysis: cleaning with heat alone, no chemicals, no scrubbing.
The cavity climbs to five hundred Celsius, nine hundred thirty Fahrenheit.
Before it gets that hot, a motorized latch locks the door.
Grease is long chains of carbon and hydrogen. At this heat, they snap apart.
The pieces escape through a vent as smoke, steam and carbon dioxide.
What can't burn stays behind: a thin grey ash you wipe off with a cloth.
Thick insulation and a triple-glazed door keep that heat inside.
Under the controls, a fan blows the whole time, for up to three hours.
The door stays locked until it cools to about two hundred fifty Celsius, four hundred eighty Fahrenheit.
It's tough on the oven too: a tired fan or thermal fuse can give up.
So the lock isn't a glitch. It's how your oven burns itself clean.
