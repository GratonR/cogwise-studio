No latch. No lock. Your fridge door is held shut by a fridge magnet.
Inside the rubber seal, a flexible magnet runs all around the door.
The seal is a hollow rubber tube that squashes like an accordion.
In its pocket sits a strip of rubber packed with magnetic powder.
The fridge cabinet is steel, so the strip grabs it on every side.
Its poles run in thin stripes, so almost all the pull is on one face.
That pull crushes the hollow seal flat against the frame. No gaps.
Shut the door on a sheet of paper, tug, and you'll feel it grip.
Old fridges had latches that only opened from outside. Kids hiding inside got trapped.
Since 1958, US law says a push from inside must open the door: sixty-seven newtons, about fifteen pounds.
A magnet does both: it holds tight, then lets go to a firm shove.
So your door isn't locked. It's stuck, like the magnets on the front.
