Carpenters smash the point of a nail. On purpose.
The secret: a sharp point splits wood. A blunt one doesn't.
Wood is a bundle of long fibres, like dry spaghetti glued side by side.
A sharp point slides between those fibres and pushes them apart, like a wedge.
Near the end of a board, that push runs along the grain, and the board cracks open.
Like an axe: the thin edge opens the log along its fibres.
Now flatten the point with two taps of the hammer.
The blunt tip can't slip between fibres. It crushes and cuts them right where it lands.
Like a hole punch: a clean plug pushed down, nothing forced sideways.
The fibres around the nail barely move, so there's nothing to start a crack.
Same board, same spot. Sharp nail: split. Blunt nail: solid.
A blunt nail cuts its way in, instead of wedging the wood apart.
