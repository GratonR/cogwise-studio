No windows to open, 400 kilometers up. So where does the air come from?
The station makes its own oxygen, out of plain water.
Run electricity through water, and it splits into oxygen and hydrogen.
The water is recycled from humidity, sweat, even the crew's urine.
Solar power runs the electrolyzer. Oxygen bubbles out into the cabin.
Every breath adds carbon dioxide. Zeolite beads trap it.
Zeolite is a mineral crystal, full of molecule-sized tunnels.
Carbon dioxide sticks to the walls. Oxygen and nitrogen slip through.
Heat the beads, and they release the carbon dioxide.
In a hot reactor, it meets the leftover hydrogen.
Out come water and methane. The water goes back to the start.
The methane is useless on board, so it's vented into space.
That methane carries hydrogen away, so only half the oxygen comes back.
So cargo ships still bring water and oxygen, plus backup oxygen candles.
The station's air is mostly yesterday's water, split by sunlight.
Now you know where every breath up there comes from. Pretty clever, right?
