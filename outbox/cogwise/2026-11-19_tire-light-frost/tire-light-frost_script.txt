First frost, and your tire light is on. Yet nothing leaked.
The secret: it's the same air. It just got cold.
Inside the tire, billions of air molecules race around and bang into the rubber.
Each tiny hit pushes outward. All those hits together are the pressure.
Cool the air, and the molecules slow down. They hit softer, and less often.
Like a ball left outside overnight: same air, but soft by morning.
Every ten Celsius colder costs about twelve kilopascals. Almost two psi.
Pumped at twenty Celsius, sixty-eight Fahrenheit. Next morning, minus five, twenty-three Fahrenheit.
About thirty kilopascals, four psi, are gone. Roughly twelve percent.
Add the slow seep every tire has, and you can cross the sensor's warning line.
Drive ten minutes, the rubber warms, the molecules speed up, and the light often goes out.
Nothing leaked. Cold air just hits the rubber softer.
