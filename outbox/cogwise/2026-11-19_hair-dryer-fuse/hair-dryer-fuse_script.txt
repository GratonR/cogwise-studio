Your hair dryer can overheat exactly once. Then it never turns on again.
The secret: a tiny thermal fuse that's built to melt.
Inside, a fan pushes air across a coil of wire glowing red-hot.
Block the air, and a bimetal strip bends away and cuts the power.
When it cools down, the strip snaps back. The dryer works again.
Right behind it, wired in line, sits a little metal can: the thermal fuse.
Inside, a solid pellet holds a spring squeezed against a sliding contact.
At its rated temperature, the pellet melts like candle wax.
The spring pushes the contact away. The circuit is open, for good.
It only acts if the thermostat fails, for example when its contacts weld shut.
Then nothing stops the heat. So the fuse kills the dryer before a fire can start.
A dead dryer often means one blown fuse: one overheat, on purpose.
