Those thin lines on your back window? Every one of them is a heater.
They're not wires. They're silver paste, printed onto the inside of the glass.
At the factory, the glass goes into an oven, and the silver fuses into its surface.
Down each side runs a wider strip of silver, called a bus bar.
Press the defrost button, and twelve volts from the battery feed one bar.
The current splits and runs across every line at once, to the other bar.
Each line is thin, so it resists the current, and that resistance becomes heat.
The glass climbs above zero Celsius, thirty-two Fahrenheit, and the ice melts outward from each line.
The lines are spaced just right, so their warm bands meet in the middle.
They're wired side by side, so one scratched line leaves just one frosty stripe.
Silver line plus current equals a heater you can see through.
