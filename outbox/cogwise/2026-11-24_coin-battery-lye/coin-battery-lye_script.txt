This battery isn't leaking. It's making lye, the stuff in drain cleaner.
And it makes it from the water in your own body.
A coin battery has two faces: plus on one side, minus on the other.
Stuck in a child's throat, both faces touch the wet lining.
That wet tissue conducts, so current flows from one face to the other.
On the minus side, electrons split water into hydrogen gas and hydroxide.
Hydroxide is lye. It piles up right against the tissue, on that narrow side.
It eats tissue the way drain cleaner eats a hair clog.
In animal tests, burns begin within fifteen minutes. A hole can form in two hours.
Lithium coin cells push three volts, twice a double-A, so they work faster.
A dead one still has enough voltage to keep making lye.
Battery plus wet tissue equals a tiny lye factory. That's why many remotes screw the battery shut.
