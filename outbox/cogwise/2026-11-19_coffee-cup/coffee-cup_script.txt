Paper falls apart in water. So why doesn't your coffee cup leak?
Because your coffee never touches the paper. It sits in plastic.
Paper is a mat of wood fibres, stuck together by tiny hydrogen bonds.
Water slips between the fibres, breaks those bonds, and the mat falls apart.
So the inside of the cup is coated with a film of polyethylene.
It's melted onto the paperboard, thinner than a human hair.
Water can't pass through it, so the fibres behind it stay dry.
And there's no glue. The side seam is sealed with that same plastic.
Hot air melts the film in a fraction of a second, welding the overlap shut.
The bottom is a separate paper disc, coated on its top side.
The wall folds under its edge, is crushed tight, and the film welds it shut.
Paper gives the cup its shape. An almost invisible plastic skin keeps it dry.
