Your elevator motor barely lifts anything. The iron behind the wall does.
It's a stack of iron blocks on the other end of the cable.
The cable runs over a grooved wheel: car on one side, iron on the other.
That counterweight equals the empty car, plus about half the load it can carry.
Take a one-thousand-kilo car, twenty-two hundred pounds, built to carry a thousand more.
Its counterweight is about fourteen hundred fifty kilos, thirty-two hundred pounds.
Like a seesaw, the two sides almost cancel. The motor only lifts the difference.
Half full, that difference is just fifty kilos, a hundred ten pounds. One passenger.
Even packed, it only hauls the extra five hundred fifty kilos, twelve hundred pounds.
And empty? The iron is heavier, so the car wants to rise on its own.
Going up empty, the motor holds it back, and newer drives turn that into electricity.
Elevator equals seesaw. The iron does the lifting; the motor just tips the balance.
