Every freight train is coupled loosely. On purpose.
That slack lets the locomotive start the cars one at a time, not all at once.
A car standing still is much harder to get moving than to keep rolling.
Starting can take about four times the pull of rolling slowly.
Each car grabs the next with a steel knuckle, like two clasped hands.
Those knuckles never fit tight. There's play, plus a spring box behind each one.
So engineers often ease back first, squeezing all that slack together.
Then they pull. Only the first car is jerked into motion.
A moment later, its own slack runs out, and it yanks the second car.
Clack, clack, clack. That sound running down the train is the slack running out.
The locomotive never fights a hundred stuck cars at once. Just one, again and again.
Coupled tight, the same engine might not budge the train at all.
