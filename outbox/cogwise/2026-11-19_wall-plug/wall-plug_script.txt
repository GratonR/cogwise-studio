Your wall is basically chalk. This plug doesn't grip it. It makes a fist.
Turn the screw, and the plug folds up behind the board, wider than the hole.
Plasterboard is gypsum between two paper sheets, twelve and a half millimetres, half an inch.
A bare screw only cuts a thread in that chalk.
Hang six kilos, thirteen pounds, and it pulls the chalk out.
The plug is a plastic sleeve with slotted legs. Its tail grabs the screw.
Turning, the screw drags the tail toward you.
The legs can't go anywhere, so they buckle outward and squash against the board.
Now the board is pinched between the screw head and that plastic fist.
Your shelf no longer tugs one thread. It presses a whole patch of board.
It holds around thirty kilos, sixty-five pounds, before the board gives up.
So your shelf doesn't hang on chalk. It hangs on a fist behind the wall.
