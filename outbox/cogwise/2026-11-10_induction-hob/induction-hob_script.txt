You can boil water on an induction hob through a sheet of paper.
And the paper won't even scorch, because the hob itself makes no heat at all.
The heat is made inside the pan, in the metal of its own base.
Under the glass hides a flat coil of copper wire.
Its magnetic field flips direction over twenty thousand times every second.
Each flip drags electric current around in little whirlpools inside the steel base.
These are eddy currents, and steel resists them hard, so the metal heats up.
So the heat appears directly under your food, not in a burner below it.
Paper and glass can't carry current, so the field passes straight through them untouched.
They only warm up by touching the pan: with water inside, about one hundred Celsius, two hundred twelve Fahrenheit.
Lift the pan away, and the heating stops in an instant.
So an induction hob never cooks anything. It turns your pan into the stove.
