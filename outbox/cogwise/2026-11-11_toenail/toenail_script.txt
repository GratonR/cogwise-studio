One slanted nail can close this gap between two boards, all by itself.
Watch closely: with every hammer blow, the top board slides a little closer.
The secret: a hammer only pushes a nail along its own length.
Drive it straight down, and all of that push goes into the wood.
But tilt the nail, and the push splits into two parts.
One part drives the nail deeper, down into the board underneath.
The other part shoves sideways, in the direction the nail is pointing.
The nail is gripped tight by the top board, so the board rides along.
At forty-five degrees, the two parts are equal, so every blow sinks and slides.
That's why framers set a stud three millimetres, an eighth of an inch, off its line.
Blow after blow, it creeps right onto the pencil mark, and the gap shuts tight.
Slanted nail: one push, split two ways — down, and together.
