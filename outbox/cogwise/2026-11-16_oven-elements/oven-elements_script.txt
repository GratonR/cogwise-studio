Your oven has no flame. It heats with up to three glowing tubes.
Each one works like a toaster wire, just much bigger and tougher.
It's a steel tube, with a resistance wire packed in mineral powder.
Current heats the wire. The powder lets heat out, but never the electricity.
The first one sits at the bottom, often hidden right under the oven floor.
It warms the floor, which radiates upward and sets warm air rising.
The second one runs along the ceiling. That's the top heater, or grill.
It beams intense heat straight down, browning the top of your food.
Bottom alone crisps a base. Top alone browns. Together, they bake evenly.
Some ovens hide a third one at the back: a coiled ring, like a snail.
It circles a fan, which pushes the hot air all around the food.
No flame anywhere. Just wires glowing hot, in exactly the right places.
