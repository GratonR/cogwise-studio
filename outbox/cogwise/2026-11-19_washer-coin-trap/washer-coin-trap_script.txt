Every coin your washer swallows ends up in one tiny pocket.

Behind the door sits a plastic filter, screwed into the drain pump.
A loose coin slips past the drum and drops into the outer tub.
When the machine drains, the water rushes down a hose, carrying the coin.
Every drop must pass through this filter before it reaches the pump.
The water flows through the grille. The coin can't, so it stays.
Right behind it spins the impeller, a small plastic fan that pushes water out.
Without the filter, one coin jams it. The pump hums, but nothing drains.
Samsung says to clean it five or six times a year. Most people never have.
To empty it: unplug, put down a towel and a shallow tray.
Drain the little hose first, then twist the filter counterclockwise and rinse it.
