Your dishwasher eats salt, but your plates never see it.
Not one grain touches your dishes. It feeds a tiny water softener under the tub.
Your tap water carries dissolved limestone, mostly calcium and magnesium.
Heated in your dishwasher, it becomes the white film on your glasses.
So before every wash, the water flows through a cartridge of tiny plastic beads.
The beads grab the calcium and magnesium, and let go of sodium instead.
After a few washes, every single bead is clogged with limestone.
That's when the salt comes in. A valve sends water into the salt box.
Out comes very salty water, called brine, and it rushes through the beads.
So much sodium pushes the limestone off, and the beads are fresh again.
Then that salty, chalky water is pumped straight down the drain.
So the salt never cleans a thing. It's just the reset button for the beads.
