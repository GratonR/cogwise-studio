The box down your street isn't buzzing.
Its steel core is stretching and shrinking, a hundred times every second.
Inside sits a core of thin silicon-steel sheets, stacked tight.
Two copper coils wrap around it, and alternating current flows through them.
That current magnetizes the steel, and magnetized steel gets slightly longer. It's called magnetostriction.
A one-metre core grows just a few thousandths of a millimetre, about a ten-thousandth of an inch.
Tiny, but over and over, the whole core pushes the air like a speaker.
In Europe, the current swings back and forth fifty times a second.
But steel stretches whichever way it's magnetized: twice per swing.
Fifty swings, two stretches each: a steady hum at one hundred hertz.
In North America it's sixty, so transformers hum at one hundred twenty.
So that hum isn't electricity leaking. It's steel, breathing to the rhythm of your power.
