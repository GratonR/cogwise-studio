Your plane window has a tiny hole in it. On purpose.
That little hole decides which pane does all the hard work.
Because a plane window isn't one window. It's three, stacked together.
The one you can touch is a thin plastic shield. It only stops scratches.
Behind it sits the middle pane. That's the one with the hole.
Then the outer pane, facing thin air eleven kilometres up, thirty-six thousand feet.
Up there, cabin air pushes outward with about four hundred fifty kilograms, a thousand pounds, on every window.
The hole lets cabin air slip past the middle pane, into the gap.
Same pressure on both sides, so the middle pane just rests.
The outer pane takes the whole push on its own, flexing very slightly.
If it ever cracks, the middle pane takes over. A spare, already in place.
Tiny hole: one pane working, one pane waiting.
